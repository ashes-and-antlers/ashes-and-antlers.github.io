Entry 01 One world, two peoples

Both peoples are born into the same procedurally generated world; every hill, river, and berry patch drawn from a single seed. They gather the same finite resources, compete for the same land, and feel the same pressures: hunger, supply, season, war.

No outcome is scripted. Both civilizations run under identical rules, and every result traces back to a cause you can find in the world's own record.

Entry 02 The two peoples

The Hearth Confederacy

Settled builders with strong institutions. Construction, agriculture, and defensive cohesion come naturally, but a disrupted supply line is a slow, quiet wound.

The Iron Swarm

Mobile, caste-based expansionists. Fast gathering, raiding, and distributed command but concentrated losses cut deep, and the soil is never tended.

You advise one people. The other answers in kind.

Entry 03 The rules of the archive

  1. 01

    A deterministic core

    Same seed, same commands, same world — every time. Reproducibility is tested, not hoped for.

  2. 02

    Simulation first

    Every visible outcome has a causal chain in simulation state. Nothing is faked to make a story.

  3. 03

    Readable complexity

    Alerts, inspectors, and overlays answer three questions: what changed, why, and what you can do.

  4. 04

    Logistics decides wars

    Armies need food, tools, medicine, transport, rest, and command. So do settlements.

Entry 04 The ledger is open

The simulation, its tests, and its balance live in the open — same source, same seed, same result. Follow the work, file a finding, or fork the world.
